How the Analytic Solution Calculators Work

Closed-form pricing gives an option or exposure value from a formula with no simulation, so results are instant and exact up to floating-point error. This page indexes QuantCalc's closed-form tools, from vanilla options and American approximations to caplets, bond options and swaptions, and the exposure calculators for potential future exposure (PFE) and expected exposure (EE) used in counterparty credit risk.

Pricing Methodology & Mathematical Formula

The foundation is the Black-Scholes formula for a European call on a non-dividend asset:

\[ C=S\,N(d_1)-Xe^{-rT}N(d_2),\quad d_{1,2}=\frac{\ln(S/X)+(r\pm\tfrac12\sigma^2)T}{\sigma\sqrt T} \]

Black-76 caplets replace the spot with a forward rate, and Hull-White bond options use a Gaussian short-rate model whose bond price is lognormal-like. Exotic formulas exist for some payoffs; the geometric Asian option is closed-form because a geometric average of lognormal prices is lognormal. For exposure, a portfolio value \(V_t\) that is normal with mean \(\mu_t\) and standard deviation \(s_t\) has

\[ \mathrm{EE}_t=\mathbb{E}[\max(V_t,0)],\qquad \mathrm{PFE}_t^{\alpha}=\mu_t+s_t\,N^{-1}(\alpha) \]

where \(\alpha\) is the confidence level, commonly 95% or 97.5%. Common inputs are spot or forward, strike, maturity, rate, volatility and, for exposure, the confidence level.

Worked Example

For a one-year call with S = 95, X = 100, r = 10% and σ = 25%: \(d_1=0.320\), \(d_2=0.070\), \(N(d_1)=0.6255\), \(N(d_2)=0.5278\), so \(C=95(0.6255)-100e^{-0.1}(0.5278)\approx 11.66\). For a normal exposure with \(\mu_t=0\) and \(s_t=10\), the 95% PFE is \(10\times1.645=16.45\). Open the Black-Scholes calculator or the EE and PFE calculator to check these figures.

Frequently Asked Questions

When can I use a closed-form formula instead of Monte Carlo?

Use closed forms when the payoff and model admit one, such as European options in Black-Scholes. Switch to trees, PDEs or Monte Carlo for early exercise, discrete barriers or path-dependent features without a formula.

What is potential future exposure?

PFE is a high percentile of the future positive exposure to a counterparty. It is used to set credit limits and capital, while EE is the average exposure.

How accurate are the American option approximations?

The Barone-Adesi-Whaley and Bjerksund-Stensland formulas are analytic approximations. They are fast and usually close to numerical benchmarks, but compare them with a tree or PDE for deep in-the-money or long-dated cases.
