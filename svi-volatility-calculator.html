Math formulas

Raw SVI total variance (Gatheral, 2004):

$$ w(k)=a+b\left(\rho\,(k-m)+\sqrt{(k-m)^2+\sigma^2}\right),\qquad k=\ln\frac{K}{F},\quad F=S_0e^{(r-q)T} $$

Implied volatility: $\sigma_{imp}(k)=\sqrt{w(k)/T}$.

Black-76 prices with the SVI volatility at each strike:

$$ C=e^{-rT}\left[F\,N(d_1)-K\,N(d_2)\right],\quad P=C-e^{-rT}(F-K),\quad d_{1,2}=\frac{-k\pm\tfrac12 w(k)}{\sqrt{w(k)}} $$

Parameter effects: ATM-ish level rises with $a$; wing slopes are $b(1\pm\rho)$ (right/left); $m$ translates the smile; $\sigma$ rounds the vertex; the minimum total variance is $a+b\sigma\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$ at $k=m+\rho\sigma/\sqrt{1-\rho^2}$.

Butterfly (static) no-arbitrage requires $w>0$ and the density function non-negative:

$$ g(k)=\left(1-\frac{k\,w'}{2w}\right)^2-\frac{w'^2}{4}\left(\frac1w+\frac14\right)+\frac{w''}{2}\ge 0 $$

with $w'=b\left(\rho+\frac{k-m}{\sqrt{(k-m)^2+\sigma^2}}\right)$ and $w''=\frac{b\sigma^2}{((k-m)^2+\sigma^2)^{3/2}}$. Lee's moment formula bounds the wings: $b(1+|\rho|)\le 4/T$.

Pros and cons of SVI

Pros

  • Only five parameters, each with a clear geometric meaning.
  • Linear wings match Lee's asymptotics for extreme strikes.
  • Fast and stable to fit; smooth curve gives smooth prices and Greeks.
  • Closed-form derivatives make arbitrage checks easy.
  • Widely used for equity and index volatility surfaces.

Cons

  • Calibration is non-convex; the fit can depend on starting values.
  • No built-in guarantee of no butterfly arbitrage: it must be checked or imposed.
  • Each maturity is fitted separately; calendar-spread arbitrage across slices needs extra constraints (e.g. SSVI).
  • Purely a fitting form: no dynamics, so it is not for pricing path-dependent products on its own.
  • Parameters can be unstable and correlated, and short-dated smiles can be hard to fit.

FAQ

What does SVI stand for?

Stochastic Volatility Inspired, a parametrization of the implied total variance smile introduced by Jim Gatheral. It mimics the large-maturity shape of the Heston smile but is a curve-fitting form, not a stochastic process.

What does each raw SVI parameter do?

a shifts the smile vertically; b controls the angle between the wings; ρ rotates the smile and sets the skew (negative ρ gives a steeper left wing, typical of equities); m shifts it horizontally; σ smooths the vertex, with small σ giving a sharp V shape.

Why use total variance instead of volatility?

Total variance $w=\sigma_{imp}^2T$ has linear wings, matches Lee's moment formula, and makes calendar arbitrage a simple monotonicity-in-maturity condition.

How do I avoid arbitrage?

Keep $w(k)>0$, make sure $g(k)\ge0$ for all $k$ (the table shows $g$ and the check above flags violations), and keep $b(1+|\rho|)\le4/T$. For surfaces, consider SSVI or add calendar constraints.

Are the option prices from a model?

No. They are Black-76 prices using the SVI implied volatility at each strike, so they reproduce the smile exactly. Prices are in the same currency units as the spot.

Can I use this for several maturities?

This tool shows one slice at a time. Change T and the parameters to inspect another slice; a full surface needs parameters per maturity that respect calendar consistency.
