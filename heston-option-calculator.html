Heston Stochastic Volatility Option Calculator

Price European calls and puts using the Heston model's characteristic function and numerical integration. All calculations run in your browser.

Why use Heston?

Black–Scholes assumes one constant volatility, so its implied volatility is flat across strikes. Heston makes variance random and correlates variance shocks with asset returns. This captures the volatility smile or skew seen across option strikes—especially the equity downside skew controlled in part by the correlation parameter \(\rho\).

Model

\( dS_t = (r-q)S_t\,dt + \sqrt{v_t}S_t\,dW_t^S \)

\( dv_t = \kappa(\theta-v_t)\,dt + \sigma_v\sqrt{v_t}\,dW_t^v,\quad d\langle W^S,W^v\rangle_t = \rho\,dt \)

European prices are computed from the Heston characteristic function using Simpson integration. The put price is obtained from put–call parity. The Black–Scholes benchmark volatility is the square root of the expected average variance over the selected maturity.

This is an educational numerical calculator, not investment advice. The reported smile is a model illustration, not a calibration to market quotes.
