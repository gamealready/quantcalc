Dupire formula and method

For a European call price surface C(K,T), with continuously compounded risk-free rate r and dividend yield q, the forward Dupire formula is:

\[ \sigma_{\mathrm{loc}}^2(K,T) = \frac{C_T + (r-q)K C_K + qC} {\frac{1}{2}K^2 C_{KK}}. \]

The inputs approximate this price surface at (K − ΔK,T), (K,T), (K + ΔK,T), (K,T − ΔT), and (K,T + ΔT). The displayed derivatives and local volatility are therefore sensitive to quote noise and step sizes. Use a smooth, arbitrage-aware implied-volatility surface and check stability by changing ΔK and ΔT. The calculator does not fit or validate a complete volatility surface.

Local Volatility FAQ

Does this use implied volatility directly as local volatility?

No. Implied volatilities are first converted into call prices. Dupire's formula uses the derivatives of those prices with respect to strike and maturity, so local and implied volatility generally differ.

Why must T be larger than ΔT?

The central maturity difference requires quotes at both T − ΔT and T + ΔT. The earlier maturity must remain positive.

What should I do if the local variance is not positive?

Check that the quotes are smooth and arbitrage-consistent, then try a less noisy surface or different finite-difference steps. A non-positive estimate is not replaced with an absolute value or clipped result.
