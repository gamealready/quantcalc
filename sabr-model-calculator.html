Model

\( dF_t = \alpha_t F_t^{\beta}\,dW_t^1,\quad d\alpha_t = \nu\,\alpha_t\,dW_t^2,\quad d\langle W^1,W^2\rangle_t=\rho\,dt \)

\( \sigma_{B}(K) \approx \dfrac{\alpha}{(FK)^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}}\left[1+\frac{(1-\beta)^2}{24}\ln^2\frac FK+\frac{(1-\beta)^4}{1920}\ln^4\frac FK\right]}\cdot\frac{z}{x(z)}\left[1+\left(\frac{(1-\beta)^2\alpha^2}{24(FK)^{1-\beta}}+\frac{\rho\beta\nu\alpha}{4(FK)^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}}}+\frac{2-3\rho^2}{24}\nu^2\right)T\right] \)

where \( z=\frac{\nu}{\alpha}(FK)^{\frac{1-\beta}{2}}\ln\frac FK \) and \( x(z)=\ln\frac{\sqrt{1-2\rho z+z^2}+z-\rho}{1-\rho} \). Option prices are Black-76 prices using this volatility.

Pros of the SABR model

  • Closed-form implied volatility: Hagan's formula makes pricing and calibration very fast.
  • Interpretable parameters: α sets the level, β the backbone, ρ the skew and ν the smile curvature.
  • Good smile fit: It fits a single-maturity smile well, which is why it is a market standard for swaptions, caps and FX options.
  • Stable hedging: Sensitivities to F, α, ρ and ν are intuitive and widely used by trading desks.
  • Flexible dynamics: β spans normal (0) to lognormal (1) behavior and, with shifts, accommodates negative rates.

Cons of the SABR model

  • Approximation error: Hagan's expansion is inaccurate for long maturities, high ν, and far-from-the-money strikes.
  • Arbitrage: The implied density can go negative at low strikes, which breaks no-arbitrage conditions and gives negative probabilities.
  • Single maturity: Parameters are fitted per expiry; there is no consistent term structure or dynamics across maturities.
  • Boundary behavior: With β<1 the forward can hit zero, so absorbing/reflecting boundary treatment matters; shifted SABR is needed for negative rates.
  • Calibration issues: α, β and ρ are partly redundant, so β is usually fixed in advance.
  • No volatility mean reversion: Volatility is a lognormal martingale, unlike Heston, so forward smiles and path-dependent products are poorly modeled.

This is an educational calculator, not investment advice, and the smile is a model illustration rather than a market calibration.
